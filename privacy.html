OneThing Support Privacy

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026

In short: OneThing doesn't collect any data. The developer has no server and can't see your tasks or anything else you store in the app. There's no account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no third-party SDKs.

Where your data is stored

Everything you add (tasks, notes, steps, reminders, repeat settings, completed tasks and, if you keep it, focus history) is stored on your device. Preferences and a running focus session stay on the device they were set on.

If you're signed in to iCloud and keep “Sync with iCloud” turned on in the app, your tasks also sync between your devices through your own private iCloud database (Apple CloudKit). That data is in your iCloud account and is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; the developer has no access to it. Without an iCloud account, or with sync turned off, your data stays only on your device (and in your device backups).

Focus history contains only when and how long you focused, never task text, and you can turn it off in Settings › Privacy.

Widgets, Live Activities and Siri

To show your current task in widgets, OneThing writes a small summary to a private storage area on your device that only OneThing and its widgets can read. The focus Live Activity is created on your device; no push service is used. Siri and Shortcuts requests are handled by OneThing on your device. When “Hide task names” or the app lock is on, task text is kept out of all of these.

How tasks are processed

Choosing your one thing and “Make it smaller” use simple rules and built-in ideas on your device. No task is sent to the developer or to any AI provider.

Network requests

OneThing itself makes no network requests. The only network traffic comes from Apple's iCloud sync, if you use it, and from “Delete OneThing data from iCloud”, if you choose it.

Permissions

  • Notifications: for reminders and the end of a focus timer, scheduled on your device. Asked when you first set a reminder or start a timed session.
  • Face ID or Touch ID: only if you turn on the app lock.
  • Live Activities: iOS asks the first time a focus session appears on your Lock Screen.

All permissions are optional. The app works without them.

Your control

  • Export: Settings › Your data › Export as JSON or CSV. The file is created on your device and goes only where you send it.
  • Delete: Settings › Your data › Delete all data removes everything from your device and, when sync is on, from your iCloud and your other devices. With sync off, “Delete OneThing data from iCloud” removes the iCloud copy and keeps the tasks on your device.
  • Turn off sync: Settings › iCloud › Sync with iCloud. Nothing on your device is deleted.
  • Deleting the app removes its data from that device. You can also manage OneThing's iCloud data in Settings › your name › iCloud.

Diagnostics

OneThing has no crash reporting or analytics of its own. If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in iOS settings, Apple may provide the developer with anonymous, aggregated crash reports and usage statistics, which contain no task content.

Children

OneThing doesn't knowingly collect data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
